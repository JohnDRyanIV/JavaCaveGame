What I Learned:

Extending from the abstract Character class is pretty interesting. There are shared traits between
an Enemy and a Player that are shown in the Character superclass, such as HP and the ability to defend.
Another implementation of extending a superclass might be a Circle and a Square extending from a shape
class. The Shape class could define an abstract method getArea(), and its implementation in Square
would be length*length whereas its implementation in Circle would be pi*radius*radius.

Regarding the changes I made to the Player, if I also wanted goblins to be able to cast spells, I 
would have added the mana and mana recharge rate variables to the Character class instead of the 
\Player class, along with the functions that allow spells to be cast and mana to regenerate. 
Instead, I decided to make spellcasting strictly available to the player.

Program Modifications:

I added a spell attack, a mana variable, and a mana recharge rate variable.
A spell has double the attack of a sword, but costs 50 mana to cast.
When upgraded, it deals +2 extra damage instad of +1.
When mana regen rate is upgraded, +10 mana is recharged per turn (max mana is 100).

I wanted to give the player more tools than just sword and defense.
Most of the challenge came from updating the output - I added a few extra cases to the switch
statements & a boolean that controls whether or not the player is able to cast a spell, adjusting
output accordingly. Display output was also changed, so the player knows how much mana they have,
and how much mana they regenerate per turn.
Implementing the mana and spellcasting logic within the player class was comparatively easy, as
I didn't have to navigate a rather dense sequence of output statements.
